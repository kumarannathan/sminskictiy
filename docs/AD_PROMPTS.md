# Ad creative prompts

Image-generation prompts for Sminski City marketing art.

**Two rules for all of them:**

1. **Every prompt carries the style block.** `.claude/rules/pipeline.md` requires
   it on every generation prompt, and `design.md` is the long form that wins any
   conflict.
2. **Never ask the generator for text.** It garbles it, and Roblox rejects ads
   with illegible copy. Each prompt below reserves a clean area — composite the
   logo and headline in afterwards.

And one that is not a rule but should be: **only advertise what is in the
build.** Every image below is grounded in something a player can actually walk
up to today. Ad-to-product mismatch is the fastest way to a high bounce rate.

## The style block

```
SMISKI CITY STYLE

Geometry:    rounded, chunky, simplified, minimal sharp edges
Proportions: oversized objects, tiny characters, slightly exaggerated
             architecture
Materials:   soft matte, subtle roughness, limited reflective surfaces
Colors:      pastel, warm, playful, occasional saturated accents
Lighting:    soft sunlight, warm interiors, colorful nighttime lighting
Detail:      high silhouette readability, medium geometric detail, lots of
             small environmental storytelling
```

## 1. The city — the core fantasy (16:9)

> A dense pastel toy city seen from low down at a street corner — cream, sage,
> butter-yellow and mint buildings meeting the pavement with no gaps, awnings
> and shop signage, mid-rise blocks with a glass tower rising behind them. A
> single tiny rounded green character stands on the kerb in the lower left,
> dwarfed by everything. Late afternoon sun, honest soft-edged shadows, gentle
> atmospheric haze fading the far end of the street. Keep the upper right third
> clear sky for a logo.

*TINY CHARACTER. BIG CITY.*

## 2. The pizzeria — "actually have a job" (1:1)

> Interior of a small pastel pizzeria: a brick oven with a warm glowing mouth on
> the right, a floured prep bench with a rolling pin, tubs of brightly coloured
> toppings, a coral-red counter. A tiny rounded green character in a chef's hat
> stands mid-room holding a pizza, mid-motion. Another small character waits at
> the counter. Warm interior light against cool daylight through the shopfront
> window. Lower third kept simple for a headline.

*GET A JOB. ACTUALLY DO IT.*

## 3. Skins — collection and expression (1:1)

> Seven tiny rounded characters in a row against a clean pale backdrop, each in
> a different full-body costume: astronaut with a clear bubble helmet, ninja in
> a dark hood with a red sash, knight in plate armour with a red plume, little
> green dinosaur with a spined crest and tail, wizard in a wide pointed hat,
> deep-sea diver with mask and flippers, and one in plain soft green. Even front
> lighting, soft shadows on the floor, strong silhouettes. Generous clean space
> above the row.

*15 LOOKS. PICK YOURS.*

## 4. Your apartment — the ownership hook (16:9)

> A cutaway view into a warm pastel apartment high in a tower — bed, rug, plant,
> a small table — with a huge window on the right looking out over a hazy pastel
> skyline at golden hour. One tiny rounded green character sits on the bed
> looking out. Warm lamp light inside, gold sunset light outside. Cosy, still,
> lived-in. Left third kept uncluttered.

*MOVE IN. IT'S YOURS.*

## 5. Driving — freedom and scale (16:9)

> Low three-quarter rear view of a chunky pastel convertible driving down a wide
> city street at golden hour, tiny rounded green character visible in the
> driver's seat. Street lamps just coming on, warm shop windows either side,
> mid-rise buildings breaking the sightline, distant towers fading into haze.
> Motion in the composition but everything crisp. Sky kept open across the top
> for a logo.

*GO ANYWHERE.*

## 6. Every job in town — the career ad (16:9)

The trick here: **depth does the lying for you, honestly.** The five careers in
the front row are the ones that are actually playable. Everything behind them
gets smaller and hazier the further back it goes, so the image reads as "endless
work" without a single promise the build cannot keep.

> A wide crowd scene of dozens of tiny rounded green characters, each one doing
> a different job, arranged in receding rows across a pastel toy city plaza at
> midday.
>
> FRONT ROW, large and sharply lit, five characters: one in a chef's hat holding
> a pizza box; one in sunglasses leaning out of a small yellow taxi; one with a
> backpack carrying a brown parcel; one in a mint beanie sweeping with a broom
> beside a bin; one in a sun hat holding a crate of vegetables.
>
> SECOND ROW, slightly smaller: a bus driver at a wheel, a police officer, a
> construction worker in a hard hat and safety vest, a firefighter with a hose,
> a baker with a tray of buns, a barista with a cup, a grill cook with a
> spatula, a grocery clerk with a shopping basket, a mechanic with a wrench, a
> photographer with a camera.
>
> BEHIND THEM, smaller and progressively hazier with distance, many more tiny
> characters with the silhouettes of other trades — ladders, crates, mail bags,
> paint rollers, watering cans, clipboards, toolboxes — rows of them fading into
> soft atmospheric haze at the back so the crowd reads as endless.
>
> Soft midday sunlight, honest shadows, every silhouette readable, pastel
> buildings framing the plaza on both sides. Keep the top fifth open sky for a
> logo and the bottom eighth clean for a headline.

*PICK A LIFE.* — or *EVERY JOB IN TOWN.*

## 7. How to play — the four-panel explainer (16:9)

An explainer needs words, and a generator cannot draw words. So this one asks
for the PANELS and the empty label strips, and the copy gets composited in
after — which is also why each panel has to read at a glance without its
caption. If a player cannot tell what a panel means with the text covered up,
the panel is wrong.

The four steps are the actual loop: arrive, work, spend, live.

> A single wide image divided into four equal vertical panels of the same toy
> city, separated by thin clean cream gutters. Each panel has an empty circular
> cream badge at its top centre and a clear empty cream strip across its bottom
> fifth. No text or lettering anywhere.
>
> PANEL 1 -- a tiny rounded green character stepping out onto a sunlit pastel
> street corner, looking up at the buildings, one arm raised. Open and
> welcoming.
>
> PANEL 2 -- the same character in a chef's hat inside a small pizzeria,
> sliding a pizza into a glowing brick oven, a customer waiting at the coral
> counter behind.
>
> PANEL 3 -- the character beside a chunky pastel convertible outside a pastel
> apartment building, holding a small key, a gold coin icon floating above the
> car.
>
> PANEL 4 -- four tiny characters in different costumes sitting together on
> grass in a park at golden hour, one waving, warm low sun and long soft
> shadows.
>
> Consistent soft midday-to-golden lighting left to right so the four panels
> read as one day. Every silhouette readable at small size.

Copy to composite into the badges and strips:

```
1  ARRIVE      Wake up in the city. Go anywhere.
2  WORK        Clock in. Cook, drive, deliver, clean.
3  SPEND       Cars, flats, outfits. It is your money.
4  LIVE        Meet everyone. Stay as long as you like.
```

## 8. The ten loader plates (16:9 x10)

These are the loading screen, not an ad: ten images at two seconds each, one
continuous day in the city, shown while the world streams in.

**Image 10 is the one that matters most.** It is the handoff -- the live
in-game camera picks the shot up from exactly that altitude and dives back
down into the real city, so it has to end high, dark-blue and cloud-adjacent
or the seam between the stills and the game will show.

**Keep the bottom 12% of every frame uncluttered** -- the loading bar sits
there. And they must look like ten frames of one film, not ten pictures.

> 1. **DAWN ABOVE THE CLOUDS** -- looking down through a soft layer of white
>    cloud, the pastel city just visible far below in blue morning light.
> 2. **THE CITY WAKES** -- high aerial of the whole toy city, cream and mint
>    and butter-yellow rooftops, early sun raking long shadows across it.
> 3. **A STREET CORNER** -- pavement level, shopfronts with awnings, planters,
>    a tiny character stepping out of a door, another walking a small dog.
> 4. **THE BAKERY WINDOW** -- warm interior glow, trays of pastel buns, a
>    character in an apron behind the counter, one customer pointing.
> 5. **THE PIZZERIA** -- a glowing brick oven, a floured bench, tubs of
>    coloured toppings, a character in a chef's hat sliding a pizza in.
> 6. **THE TAXI** -- a chunky yellow taxi at a kerb, a character in sunglasses
>    at the wheel, a passenger stepping in, mid-morning light.
> 7. **BUILDING SOMETHING** -- a small construction site behind hoardings,
>    characters in hard hats carrying crates, a half-finished pastel building.
> 8. **HOME** -- a cosy apartment interior, bed, rug, plant, a character on the
>    floor, huge window with the skyline beyond, afternoon light.
> 9. **THE PARK AT GOLDEN HOUR** -- four or five characters sitting together on
>    grass, long warm shadows, low sun through trees, one waving.
> 10. **NIGHT, AND BACK UP** -- the city from high above after dark, warm
>    windows and small coloured signs glowing in a grid, deep blue sky, a few
>    clouds catching the last light. Composed so the camera could keep rising
>    into the cloud layer.

Export at **1024 x 576** (Roblox caps image uploads at 1024 on the long edge),
upload them, and paste the ids into `STILLS` in `SminskiTitle.client.lua` --
the slots are already numbered to match.

## 9. Three TikTok clips for Veo (9:16, 8 seconds each)

Each block is self-contained -- paste it whole. The style block is folded in,
because Veo gets one prompt and nothing else. Same two rules as the stills:
**no text in the generation** (captions go on in the editor), and **only show
what is in the build**.

Cut 2-3 seconds of real captured gameplay onto the end of every clip. A
TikTok that is all render and no game is the ad-to-product mismatch this file
warns about at the top.

### 9a. The scale reveal -- the hook

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, pastel cream, sage, mint and butter-yellow buildings, soft natural
> afternoon sunlight with honest soft-edged shadows and gentle atmospheric
> haze.
>
> Start in extreme close-up at pavement level on a single tiny rounded soft
> green character with a minimal face -- two small dot eyes -- standing at the
> edge of a kerb that comes up to its waist. It looks up. The camera pulls
> smoothly back and rises in one continuous move, and keeps going: the kerb,
> then a bench towering over it, then shopfronts with awnings, then mid-rise
> pastel buildings, then a glass tower behind them, until the character is a
> tiny green dot in a huge dense street. Hold the wide for the last second.
>
> One continuous shot, no cuts, smooth cinematic camera. Audio: soft city
> ambience, a gentle rising musical swell that lands on the wide shot. No
> dialogue, no text, no subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

On-screen text: *POV: you are 3 inches tall* → *and the whole city is yours*

### 9b. A day in the city -- the loop

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, pastel warm colours, natural lighting. The same tiny rounded soft
> green character with two small dot eyes appears in all four shots. Four
> quick shots of two seconds each, cut on the beat:
>
> SHOT 1, morning -- the character sits up in bed in a cosy pastel apartment,
> a huge window behind it showing a hazy skyline in cool early light.
> SHOT 2, midday -- wearing a chef's hat inside a small pizzeria, it slides a
> pizza on a wooden peel into a brick oven with a warm glowing mouth.
> SHOT 3, afternoon -- it drives a chunky pastel convertible down a wide city
> street, shopfronts passing, low three-quarter rear camera following.
> SHOT 4, golden hour -- it sits on the grass in a park beside three other tiny
> characters in different costumes, one waves, long warm shadows.
>
> The light moves from cool morning to warm sunset across the four shots so
> they read as one day. Audio: an upbeat cosy lo-fi beat with a clear pulse
> every two seconds, light sound effects -- a yawn-like chirp, an oven
> whoosh, a car hum, soft laughter-like chirps. No dialogue, no text, no
> subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

On-screen text, one word per shot: *WAKE UP* · *CLOCK IN* · *CASH OUT* · *HANG OUT*

### 9c. The outfit switch -- the collection

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium collectible toy. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, soft even studio-like daylight, a clean pastel city street softly
> out of focus behind.
>
> A tiny rounded soft green character with two small dot eyes walks towards
> the camera down the middle of a pavement, centred in frame, camera tracking
> backwards at its eye level at the same speed. On every second step it gives
> a small hop, and on landing its whole body has instantly changed into a
> different full-body costume, with a small soft puff of sparkles: first an
> astronaut with a clear bubble helmet, then a ninja in a dark hood with a red
> sash, then a knight in plate armour with a red plume, then a small green
> dinosaur with a spined crest and tail, then a wizard in a wide pointed hat.
> On the final hop it lands back as the plain green character and stops,
> looking straight into the camera.
>
> One continuous shot, steady walking rhythm, each change exactly on the
> landing. Audio: a bouncy playful beat, a soft pop sound on every change. No
> dialogue, no text, no subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

On-screen text: *which one are you?* -- a comment-bait question, which is what
the format is for.

### 9d. The closer -- the same end card on every clip

**Veo does not draw the logo or the words.** It cannot reproduce a logo it has
never seen, and it garbles lettering -- a closer with a melted wordmark on
every video is worse than no closer. So this prompt makes the *plate*: a calm
background with two deliberately empty zones, and the real logo
(`art/preview/logo_city.png`) and the call to action go on in the editor.

It is 8 seconds because that is what Veo makes; **use 3 of them**. All the
movement happens in the first two seconds and then it holds still, so any
3-second window from 0:01 onwards works and the text sits over a steady frame.

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, pastel cream, sage, mint and butter-yellow buildings, warm golden
> hour sunlight, gentle atmospheric haze.
>
> Locked-off camera, no camera movement at all. The top half of the frame is
> clean open sky -- a soft gradient from pale blue to warm peach with two or
> three small soft clouds drifting very slowly -- and nothing else enters it.
> Across the lower-middle of the frame, a soft out-of-focus pastel toy skyline.
> In the bottom third, in sharp focus, a rounded cream rooftop ledge runs the
> full width of the frame.
>
> In the first second a single tiny rounded soft green character with two
> small dot eyes hops up onto the ledge from below, slightly left of centre,
> lands with a small soft squash, and a small burst of pastel confetti and
> sparkles pops behind it. It turns to face the camera and waves twice. By the
> two second mark it settles into a gentle idle -- a slow breathing bob, an
> occasional blink -- and stays there for the rest of the clip while the
> confetti drifts down and clears. Nothing else moves.
>
> Audio: a short bright three-note chime that resolves within the first two
> seconds, then quiet warm ambience. No dialogue, no text, no lettering, no
> signs, no subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

Composite, top to bottom:

```
10% - 38%    the SMINSKI CITY logo (art/preview/logo_city.png), pop in at 0:01
40% - 48%    EARLY ACCESS NOW!       -- big, cream with a dark outline
50% - 55%    PLAY FREE ON ROBLOX  ·  search "Sminski City"
below that   the character on its ledge -- no text
```

Keep the third line: a TikTok viewer cannot click through to Roblox, so the
name they have to type is the actual call to action. All three sit in the
sky half on purpose -- TikTok's own buttons cover the right-hand 15% of the
frame and its caption covers the bottom 20%, so nothing that has to be read
goes there.

The three-note chime is on purpose: the same sound at the end of every video
is what makes the fourth one recognisable before the logo appears.

### 9d-2. The closer, with the store page sliding in

Same plate idea as 9d, two changes: the clean sky is now the top **two
thirds** (the store screenshot needs somewhere to land), and the character
**reacts to it** -- looks up, bounces, points -- so the card feels like part of
the scene rather than a sticker on top of it.

The screenshot itself is slid in by the editor, not drawn by Veo. It is a
store page: a garbled title or a melted play button on it reads as a fake
listing, which is the one thing an ad for a real listing cannot look like.

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, pastel cream, sage, mint and butter-yellow buildings, warm golden
> hour sunlight, gentle atmospheric haze.
>
> Locked-off camera, no camera movement at all. The top two thirds of the
> frame is clean open sky -- a soft gradient from pale blue to warm peach with
> two or three small soft clouds drifting very slowly near the edges -- and
> nothing else ever enters it. Below that, a soft out-of-focus pastel toy
> skyline. Across the bottom quarter, in sharp focus, a rounded cream rooftop
> ledge runs the full width of the frame.
>
> 0-1s: a single tiny rounded soft green character -- two small eyes and a
> tiny smile, exactly as in the reference image -- hops up onto the ledge from
> below, slightly left of centre, lands with a small soft squash, and a small
> burst of pastel confetti and sparkles pops behind it.
> 1-2s: it faces the camera and waves twice.
> 2-3s: it notices something above it: looks straight up at the empty sky
> over its head, gives two quick excited bounces, then raises one arm and
> points straight up at that empty space.
> 3-8s: it holds the point, looking up, with a slow breathing bob and an
> occasional blink. Once, around the 5 second mark, it glances at the camera
> and back up. The confetti drifts down and clears. Nothing else moves, and
> the sky above it stays completely empty the whole time.
>
> Audio: a short bright three-note chime that resolves within the first two
> seconds, then quiet warm ambience. No dialogue, no text, no lettering, no
> signs, no cards, no screens, no subtitles, no logos, no watermarks.

Use the first **5 seconds**. In the editor, top to bottom:

```
0:00.5   8% - 26%   SMINSKI CITY logo (art/preview/logo_city.png), pop in
0:01    28% - 34%   EARLY ACCESS NOW!      cream, dark outline
0:02.5  37% - 66%   the store screenshot, slides UP from under the frame
                    and lands with a small overshoot. 78% of the frame wide,
                    sitting LEFT (x 5% -> 83%) to clear TikTok's buttons.
                    Round the corners, soft drop shadow, tilt it 2-3 degrees.
0:03    68% - 72%   search "Sminski City" on Roblox
0:05    cut
```

0:02.5 is when the character starts pointing, so the card arrives where it is
already looking. Add a soft whoosh + pop under the landing; give the blue play
button one slow pulse (scale 100 -> 106 -> 100) while it holds.

Crop the screenshot to **thumbnail + title + the blue play button** and leave
off the "By" and maturity lines -- they are small print that nobody can read at
phone size, and the play button is the point.

CapCut: Overlay -> add the screenshot -> Animation -> In -> "Slide up" (0.4s).

### 9e / 9f. Two talking skits (9:16, 8 seconds, uses the Veo skin references)

Gold, Lavender, Glow, Ghost and Ninja are loaded into Veo as reference
images, so these prompts call them by those names and do not re-describe them.

Two things that are specific to dialogue:

- **8 seconds is about 20 spoken words.** Both scripts are cut to fit; adding a
  line means Veo rushes or drops one.
- **The face is tiny: two small eyes and a small smile, and that is all.** Left
  alone, Veo enlarges the mouth so it has something to lip-sync, and the
  character stops looking like ours. Both prompts pin the face to the
  reference images and say how they talk instead: a bob and a gesture on
  every line, the mouth staying small.

#### 9e. "So you work for me now"  -- Gold + Lavender · sells jobs and owning a business

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials, pastel colours, soft natural afternoon sunlight. One continuous
> shot, locked-off camera at the characters' eye level, no cuts.
>
> Setting: the pavement outside a small pastel pizzeria with a striped awning
> and a warm glowing window. The Gold character stands on the left with a
> chunky pastel convertible parked behind it. The Lavender character stands on
> the right wearing a white chef's hat and holding a pizza box. Both are tiny
> rounded toy characters whose faces stay exactly as in the reference images:
> two small eyes and a tiny smile, nothing more. The mouth never grows and
> never lip-syncs. They talk like vinyl toys: a small bounce and a hand
> gesture on each line, a head tilt when listening.
>
> 0-2s: Gold gestures grandly at the pizzeria and says in a smug, tiny,
> high-pitched voice: "I just bought the pizzeria."
> 2-4s: Lavender, flat and unimpressed, in a tiny deadpan voice: "I work at
> the pizzeria."
> 4-6s: Gold leans in, delighted: "So you work for me now."
> 6-8s: Lavender looks at the camera for a beat, sets the pizza box down on
> the pavement, says "I quit," and walks out of frame. Gold stares at the box.
>
> Audio: the two voices clear and close, quiet city ambience, a single comedic
> soft "boop" as the box touches the ground. No music. No subtitles, no
> captions, no on-screen text, no logos, no watermarks.

On-screen text (top of frame, the whole clip): *POV: your friend buys the place you work*

#### 9f. "Nobody can see us"  -- Ninja + Glow + Ghost · sells the skins

> Vertical 9:16 video, 8 seconds, stylised 3D animation that looks like a
> premium miniature toy city. Rounded chunky simplified geometry, soft matte
> materials. Night: deep blue sky, warm lit windows, one soft pink neon sign
> glowing out of frame. One continuous shot, locked-off camera at the
> characters' eye level, no cuts.
>
> Setting: a narrow pastel alley between two buildings. The Ninja character
> crouches low behind a rounded dumpster on the left, peeking round it. The
> Glow character crouches right beside it, glowing softly. All characters are
> tiny rounded toy characters whose faces stay exactly as in the reference
> images: two small eyes and a tiny smile, nothing more. The mouth never grows
> and never lip-syncs. They talk like vinyl toys: a small bounce and a hand
> gesture on each line.
>
> 0-3s: Ninja, without looking round, in an urgent tiny whisper: "Stay
> hidden. Nobody can see us."
> 3-5s: Glow, cheerful and far too loud: "Got it!" -- and as it says it, its
> glow brightens until it lights up the whole alley like a lamp.
> 5-6s: Ninja slowly turns its head to look at Glow. Silence.
> 6-8s: the Ghost character drifts calmly out through the solid wall behind
> them, semi-transparent, gives a small wave and says in a soft, friendly
> voice: "Hi guys." Ninja drops its head in defeat.
>
> Audio: the voices clear and close, quiet night ambience with distant
> crickets and a faint neon hum, a soft rising shimmer as the glow brightens.
> No music. No subtitles, no captions, no on-screen text, no logos, no

On-screen text: *when the ninja brings THAT friend* → on the Ghost's line, *15 skins. choose wisely.*

## Format notes

Roblox's Sponsored units mostly pull your **experience thumbnail (16:9)** and
**icon (square)** rather than free-form banners, so 1 / 4 / 5 / 6 double as
thumbnails and 2 / 3 work as icon art. Check the current specs on the Creator
Hub before committing — the ad formats there have changed more than once.
